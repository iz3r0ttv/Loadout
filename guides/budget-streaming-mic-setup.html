Budget Streaming Mic Setup Under $250

Prices last checked August 14, 2026 — always confirm current pricing on Amazon before buying.

You don't need a $700 broadcast mic to sound good on stream. Most of what people notice is whether your audio is clean, consistent, and not clipping — and that's a function of mic + interface + placement, not price. Here's a setup that sounds legitimately good for under $250 all-in, plus what to upgrade first if you outgrow it.

1. The mic

A dynamic cardioid mic rejects room noise and keyboard clatter far better than a cheap USB condenser, which is why most working streamers use one. The MV7 uses the same capsule as Shure's much pricier SM7dB — you're mainly giving up the SM7dB's USB-C output and touch panel, not sound quality.

Budget Pick

Shure MV7

Dynamic cardioid capsule shared with the SM7dB, USB + XLR output, built-in headphone monitoring.

~$199 View on Amazon →

2. The interface

If you go the XLR route, you need an audio interface between the mic and your PC. The Scarlett 2i2 is the industry-standard budget choice for a reason — clean preamps, simple mixer, and it'll outlast several mic upgrades.

Budget Pick

Focusrite Scarlett 2i2 (4th Gen)

2 combo XLR/TRS inputs, clean gain up to +69dB, no onboard mixing — you'll route levels in OBS or your DAW.

$179.99 View on Amazon →

3. The boom arm

Skip the desk stand. A boom arm keeps the mic close to your mouth for better rejection and gets it out of your camera frame between takes.

Budget Pick

Samson MBA28

Spring-tension boom arm, desk clamp mount — no internal cable routing, so expect a visible cable run.

~$40 View on Amazon →

When to upgrade

If the visible boom-arm cable bugs you or you outgrow the 2i2's two inputs, the next steps up are a boom arm with internal cable routing (like the RØDE PSA1+) and a multi-input interface with onboard mixing (like the RØDECaster Pro II) — both featured in the full gear list as what we actually run day-to-day.
